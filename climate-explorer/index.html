What is in a climate data file?

Most climate data comes as NetCDF files (.nc). A file holds one or more variables (e.g. temperature tas, precipitation pr) stored on a grid of dimensions: usually time × latitude × longitude, sometimes also a vertical level. Each grid box holds one number. The attributes say what the numbers mean (units, long name, missing-value flag).

Where does the data come from?

  • Reanalysis (ERA5, NCEP/NCAR, MERRA-2): past weather observations blended with a weather model, giving a complete, gridded picture of the atmosphere for every month since the 1940s–1970s.
  • Observational products (GPCP rain, ERSST sea-surface temperature): built mostly from satellites, ships, buoys and rain gauges.
  • Climate models (CMIP6, CMIP7): simulations of the climate system. historical runs cover 1850–2014; scenario runs (SSP1-2.6 … SSP5-8.5) project the future.

The four views

  • One month — a single snapshot. Press ▶ to animate through time.
  • Average — the mean over selected months and years. A 30-year average is called a climatology (“normal” climate).
  • Trend — how fast things change, from a straight-line fit through the yearly means (units per decade).
  • Variability — how much one year differs from the next (standard deviation).

Anomalies and baselines

An anomaly is the difference from a reference average, the baseline (by default 1991–2020, the current WMO normal period). Anomalies remove the big, expected patterns (tropics are hot, poles are cold, summers are warmer than winters) so the unusual part stands out: El Niño, heat waves, droughts, long-term warming. Blue = below normal, red = above normal.

Things to try

  • Sea surface temperature sample → One month → Dec 1997 or Dec 2015 → tick anomaly, region Tropical Pacific: a strong El Niño.
  • Precipitation → Average → months JJAS (monsoon) → region South Asia: the Indian summer monsoon.
  • Air temperature → Trend → Annual, 1979–2024: where is it warming fastest? Then click the Arctic.
  • Time series → From Niño 3.4, Anomaly, Bars: the history of El Niño and La Niña.
  • Switch on Climate stripes for a global-average temperature series.

Getting your own data

  • ERA5: Copernicus Climate Data Store — choose NetCDF as the format.
  • CMIP6: ESGF search — monthly Amon tables (tas, pr, psl …).
  • NOAA PSL: gridded datasets (where the samples come from).

Tips for big or awkward files

The browser keeps the whole file in memory, so keep files below ~1 GB. Cut them down with CDO:

cdo sellonlatbox,60,100,0,40 in.nc region.nc      # a lat/lon box
cdo selyear,1980/2020 in.nc years.nc               # some years
cdo monmean daily.nc monthly.nc                    # daily → monthly
cdo remapbil,r360x180 ocean_model.nc regular.nc    # curvilinear → 1° grid
cdo -f nc copy era5.grib era5.nc                   # GRIB → NetCDF
